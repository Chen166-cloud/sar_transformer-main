\title{SAR Despeckling via Fourier Refinement and\\
Representation-Consistent Reconstruction}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Speckle obscures radiometric contrast and structural detail in synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images, while models trained with simulated noise often transfer poorly to real observations. This paper develops a transparent extension of the public Trans-SAR backbone for normalized-intensity despeckling. The method inserts full-spectrum Fourier residual refinement at the bottleneck and five decoder resolutions, processes real and imaginary spectral components with shared real-valued channel mixing, and predicts a bounded log-intensity image. The prediction is analytically returned to the intensity domain before a lightweight residual compensator combines it with the original observation, avoiding arithmetic between incompatible representations. A masked post-adaptation stage further calibrates the reconstruction modules on real SAR data without clean targets while freezing the inherited Transformer encoder. On 8,400 simulated test pairs from all 21 UCMerced classes and four look numbers, one blind model obtains a macro PSNR of 28.14~dB and a macro SSIM of 0.7791. Removing all Fourier blocks lowers the two measures by 1.68~dB and 0.0179. On 592 parent-disjoint real patches, adaptation reduces the M-index from 1.8213 to 1.3365 and increases the edge-preservation index from 0.4229 to 0.8214. These results support representation-consistent spectral refinement and lightweight real-data calibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) offers day-and-night, weath\-er-independent observation, but signal-dependent speckle degrades homogeneous contrast and competes with edges and bright scatterers. Unit-mean Gamma noise supports controlled multilook simulation, whereas focused real SAR also contains correlation, processing artifacts, and scene-dependent statistics. Despeckling must therefore suppress fluctuations without erasing structure or assuming unavailable clean targets~\cite{fracastoro2021overview}.

Lee filters use local statistics~\cite{lee1980}; SAR-BM3D exploits nonlocal recurrence but requires noise information~\cite{parrilli2012}. CNNs learn paired image priors~\cite{chierchia2017,ko2022}, while Trans-SAR and later Transformers introduce long-range, multiscale, gated, or noise-guided processing~\cite{perera2022,shen2025,zhang2025}. Yet spatial reconstruction must still separate speckle from structures with overlapping frequency content.

Frequency-aware networks and transformed diffusion provide alternative representations~\cite{zhao2024,ma2024}. We focus on two implementation constraints: full-spectrum complex mixing is not a high/low-band decomposition, and a log-domain estimate should be inverted before intensity-domain fusion. For real-data transfer, multitemporal, blind-spot, masked, and intensity-only methods reduce clean-target dependence under different assumptions~\cite{dalsasso2021,molini2022,lin2025,chen2026}. We therefore use masking only for post-adaptation and do not claim unbiased blind-spot learning.

We make three contributions: (1) a public Trans-SAR extension with real--imaginary Fourier residual refinement (FDR) at the bottleneck and five decoder scales; (2) bounded log prediction with exact inversion before residual intensity compensation; and (3) a blind four-look evaluation plus encoder-frozen masked adaptation on a parent-disjoint real-SAR split. Inherited Trans-SAR components are identified explicitly; FDR denotes full-spectrum coordinate-wise mixing, guidance an unsupervised latent map, and adaptation masked calibration rather than a new blind-spot architecture.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Model-Based and Supervised Despeckling}
Lee filtering estimates local statistics under a multiplicative-noise model~\cite{lee1980}, whereas SAR-BM3D combines nonlocal grouping with transform-domain shrinkage~\cite{parrilli2012}. Deep CNNs learn paired mappings~\cite{chierchia2017}; SAR-CAM uses continuous multiscale attention~\cite{ko2022}; and Trans-SAR couples a five-stage spatial-reduction Transformer encoder with convolutional reconstruction~\cite{perera2022}. Later systems add dynamic gates and noise-guided multiscale fusion~\cite{shen2025,zhang2025}. These modules establish the value of hierarchical processing, but do not remove the tension between smoothing speckle and retaining fine structure. We therefore extend rather than replace the public Trans-SAR backbone, and attribute its encoder and basic reconstruction operators explicitly.

\subsection{Frequency- and Representation-Aware Restoration}
SAR-FDD decomposes frequency information into interacting branches~\cite{zhao2024}, while conditional diffusion with a Log-Yeo--Johnson transform addresses multiplicative statistics generatively~\cite{ma2024}. Unlike explicit high/low-band decomposition, our block applies shared real-valued channel mixing to concatenated real and imaginary coefficients at every rFFT coordinate, then couples the spatial result with a local branch. The associated output path is deterministic and analytically invertible rather than a second restoration network.

\subsection{Learning from Real SAR Observations}
Real-data methods operate under different assumptions. SAR2SAR uses multitemporal acquisitions~\cite{dalsasso2021}, whereas Speckle2Void combines a blind-spot likelihood with decorrelation~\cite{molini2022}. Speckle2Self uses masking and attention~\cite{lin2025}. SDS-SAR constructs intensity-only pairs with correlation-aware sampling~\cite{chen2026}, and SDUDNet learns from unpaired clean and noisy data~\cite{bo2025}. Our lighter masked stage freezes the inherited encoder and calibrates reconstruction from single noisy images; it is deployment adaptation, not a new blind-spot theory.

\section{Proposed Method}
\subsection{Observation Model and Bounded Log Representation}
Let \(\vect{X}\) denote a normalized clean intensity proxy and \(\vect{Y}\) its observed counterpart. Controlled synthetic experiments use independent unit-mean Gamma speckle \(\vect{N}\) with equivalent number of looks \(L\). Clipping is applied after multiplication:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{Y} &= \operatorname{clip}(\vect{X}\odot\vect{N},0,1),\\
N_{ij} &\sim \operatorname{Gamma}(L,L^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
Before clipping, \(\mathbb{E}[N_{ij}]=1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(N_{ij})=L^{-1}\). Equation~\eqref{eq:observation} is used only to construct controlled pairs; it is not asserted as an exact formation model for focused real SAR. The network input is transformed using \(\alpha=10\):
\begin{equation}
T_{\alpha}(\vect{Y})=
\frac{\log(1+\alpha\vect{Y})}{\log(1+\alpha)},
\qquad \alpha=10,
\label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
with the exact inverse
\begin{equation}
T_{\alpha}^{-1}(\widehat{\vect{Z}})=
\frac{\exp[\widehat{\vect{Z}}\log(1+\alpha)]-1}{\alpha}.
\label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
This bounded \(\log1p\) mapping compresses bright scatterers and partially reduces the signal dependence of speckle, but it does not strictly convert multiplicative speckle into additive Gaussian noise.

\subsection{Trans-SAR-Derived Reconstruction Path}
The transformed observation enters the inherited five-stage Trans-SAR encoder. Channel widths are 32, 64, 128, 320, and 512 from \(128^2\) to \(8^2\); heads are 1, 1, 2, 4, and 8, with depths 3, 3, 4, 6, and 3. These settings, overlap embedding, spatial-reduction attention, and basic upsampling are inherited from Trans-SAR~\cite{perera2022}. The log input also feeds a lightweight guidance generator.

A local--spectral bottleneck refines the deepest feature. Each decoder stage then performs upsampling, skip fusion where available, FDR, residual convolution, and guidance modulation. A sigmoid head predicts bounded log intensity; after exact inversion, a shallow compensator estimates an intensity residual from the observation and preliminary restoration.

The bottleneck combines Fourier and local depthwise-convolution features before a residual projection whose scale is initialized to 0.1. Decoder FDR blocks follow the same principle at progressively finer resolutions but use stage-specific parameters. Coarse features therefore receive global spectral correction, while skip-fused fine features recover local boundaries. After the inverse FFT, the remaining decoder operations are ordinary real-valued convolutions.

\subsection{Full-Spectrum Fourier Residual Refinement}
For an input feature \(\vect{F}\), the spatial branch applies a depthwise \(3\times3\) convolution, a pointwise projection, and GELU. In parallel, an orthonormal two-dimensional real FFT produces \(\vect{Q}\). Its real and imaginary parts are concatenated along channels:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{Q} &= \operatorname{rFFT2}(\vect{F}),\\
\vect{Q}_{c} &= [\operatorname{Re}(\vect{Q}),
                   \operatorname{Im}(\vect{Q})].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fft}
\end{equation}
Two real-valued \(1\times1\) convolutions with an intermediate GELU mix channels and real--imaginary components independently at every frequency coordinate. Splitting the result and applying the inverse real FFT gives the spectral feature \(\vect{F}_{f}\). The spatial feature \(\vect{F}_{s}\) and \(\vect{F}_{f}\) are concatenated, projected, and added to the input:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{U} &= \operatorname{GELU}
 \bigl(\vect{W}_{o,1} * [\vect{F}_{s},\vect{F}_{f}]\bigr),\\
\operatorname{FDR}(\vect{F})
 &= \vect{F}+\vect{W}_{o,2}*\vect{U}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fdr}
\end{equation}
One block is used in the bottleneck and five at decoder resolutions \(16^2\), \(32^2\), \(64^2\), \(128^2\), and \(256^2\). As Fig.~\ref{fig:fdr} emphasizes, the \(1\times1\) weights share channel mixing over frequency coordinates but do not convolve across adjacent frequencies. The inverse real FFT maps the mixed coefficients back to a real feature before spatial fusion. We therefore describe this operation as full-spectrum real--imaginary refinement, not high-frequency enhancement, magnitude--phase processing, band decomposition, or complex-valued convolution.

  \caption{Full-spectrum Fourier residual refinement (FDR). A depthwise--pointwise spatial branch complements a spectral branch. \subsection{Guidance Modulation and Intensity Compensation}
The log input produces an unsupervised single-channel guidance map. At each decoder scale, the resized map is concatenated with the feature and passed through pointwise and \(3\times3\) convolutions to a \(\tanh\) modulation, scaled by a learnable factor initialized to 0.1. With no noise-map or look-number supervision, it is latent guidance rather than a noise estimator. Let \(\widehat{\vect{Z}}\) be the sigmoid prediction and \(\widetilde{\vect{X}}=T_{\alpha}^{-1}(\widehat{\vect{Z}})\). A 2-to-32-to-32-to-1 compensator receives \(\vect{Y}\) and \(\widetilde{\vect{X}}\), with \(\gamma_c\) initialized to 0.1:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{R}_{c} &= F_{\mathrm{comp}}
 ([\vect{Y},\widetilde{\vect{X}}]),\\
\widehat{\vect{X}} &= \operatorname{clip}
 (\widetilde{\vect{X}}+\gamma_c\vect{R}_{c},0,1).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:compensation}
\end{equation}
The compensator comprises two \(3\times3\), 32-channel GELU layers and a single-channel residual head. Both operands in~\eqref{eq:compensation} are intensities, so the historical name ``dual-domain fusion'' denotes a limited post-inversion correction, not two restoration branches or direct log/intensity addition. Conditioning on both the observation and inverse-mapped estimate permits a small correction of radiometric contrast and bright-scatterer neighborhoods without asking the compensator to repeat despeckling independently. Clamping follows the residual update, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:representation_ams}(a).

\subsection{Supervised Training and Masked Post-Adaptation}
The supervised objective is defined on normalized linear intensity:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}=
\lVert\widehat{\vect{X}}-\vect{X}\rVert_2^2
+5\!\times\!10^{-7}\operatorname{TV}(\widehat{\vect{X}}).
\label{eq:supervised}
\end{equation}
For real data, a Bernoulli mask \(\vect{M}\) removes 20\% of observed pixels, with \(\vect{Y}_{m}=\vect{Y}\odot(1-\vect{M})\). The model regresses the original noisy values only at masked positions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AMS}}={}&
\frac{\lVert\vect{M}\odot[f_{\theta}(\vect{Y}_{m})-\vect{Y}]\rVert_1}
{\lVert\vect{M}\rVert_1+\epsilon}\\
&+10^{-3}\operatorname{TV}(f_{\theta}(\vect{Y}_{m})).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ams}
\end{equation}
During eight adaptation epochs at a learning rate of \(10^{-6}\), the inherited Transformer encoder is frozen while the bottleneck, decoder, guidance branch, output head, and compensator remain trainable. Training masks are stochastic; one fixed mask per validation sample is reused for checkpoint selection so the validation target does not change between epochs. Inference uses the complete unmasked image. Freezing limits drift in the representation learned from controlled synthetic data while allowing output statistics to adjust. Because the target remains noisy and focused speckle may be spatially correlated, this is constrained post-adaptation rather than an independence guarantee. Fig.~\ref{fig:representation_ams}(b) summarizes the AMS training and unmasked inference paths.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Datasets and Evaluation Protocol}
For supervised development, we use the optical NWPU-RESISC45 collection~\cite{cheng2017}. Selecting 20 training and 5 validation sources per class yields 900 and 225 independent images. RGB is converted to grayscale, and squared grayscale is the clean intensity proxy. A deterministic 100,000-step online schedule balances \(L\in\{1,2,4,8\}\) with 25,000 presentations per look; validation uses four fixed realizations per source (900 pairs). Paired flips and right-angle rotations require no interpolation.

External testing uses all 2,100 UCMerced sources across 21 classes~\cite{yang2010}, each corrupted once per look to give 8,400 fixed pairs. UCM data are excluded from model selection. This evaluates transfer between optical remote-sensing collections under simulated Gamma speckle, not real-SAR generalization. Gamma noise is sampled before clipping, and no additive, impulse, shift, stripe, or gain corruption is added.

Real data are normalized intensity patches from Mendeley dataset fs455tz88y version~1~\cite{vasquez2024data}. Only \texttt{Noisy} is used; GT16 contributes to neither adaptation, model selection, measurement, nor visualization. The 1,469 parent images split into 1,175/146/148 train/validation/test parents, corresponding to 4,700/584/592 child patches. Parent grouping ensures that four children from one \(512\times512\) image cannot cross splits. The test is parent-disjoint within one acquisition, not cross-region or cross-sensor.

Identical test IDs are used for all methods. Unsigned 8-bit inputs are divided by 255, while normalized floating-point inputs are retained; per-image percentile normalization is excluded because it would alter relative intensity scales.

\subsection{Implementation, Baselines, and Metrics}
All learned models use batch size one and Adam with learning rate \(10^{-3}\), weight decay \(10^{-5}\), and 100,000 successful updates. Validation occurs every 5,000 updates; the lowest class-by-look macro intensity MSE selects the checkpoint. One proposed-model checkpoint handles all four \(L\). Lee-MMSE uses a window selected on NWPU validation, while SAR-BM3D receives nominal \(L\) and follows its square-root-input/squared-output chain; both are \emph{oracle-\(L\)}. SAR-CAM~\cite{ko2022}, the inherited Trans-SAR baseline~\cite{perera2022}, and our model receive only corrupted intensity. All outputs are measured in normalized linear intensity without display stretching.

For the four-look UCM-21 benchmark, PSNR and SSIM are computed per image and averaged equally across classes and looks. For the 592-patch real-SAR benchmark, five nonoverlapping \(32\times32\) regions are selected once from each noisy input on a fixed grid by the lowest squared coefficient of variation after rejecting dark, zero-dominated, or saturated candidates; the same 2,960 regions are reused for all methods. ENL is a smoothness diagnostic rather than a one-direction quality score. The M-index follows ratio-image first-order and texture residuals~\cite{gomez2017}, while EPI uses fixed adjacent differences~\cite{ma2024epi}. ENL and ratio behavior are interpreted jointly because smoothing may inflate ENL while erasing structure~\cite{vasquez2025ratio}; whole-image M-index and EPI provide complementary checks.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations bound our claims. Optical NWPU and UCM sources make the synthetic results a controlled Gamma-restoration test, not a model of complete real-SAR statistics. The parent-disjoint real patches come from one acquisition, precluding cross-region or cross-sensor claims. Noisy-target masking is not theoretically unbiased under correlated speckle, so the adaptation evidence is empirical. We also report neither parameter count, operation count, runtime, nor variation across initializations and therefore make no efficiency or uncertainty-calibrated claim.

Future work should add independent acquisitions and paired source-cluster intervals for UCM and parent-cluster intervals for real SAR, without treating four looks or sibling patches as independent observations. Accordingly, the evidence supports fixed-Gamma transfer across the two optical datasets, the representation/FDR ablation under a shared budget, and within-collection masked calibration. It does not establish universal speckle statistics, absolute radiometric fidelity, or state-of-the-art performance across unmatched training pipelines.

\section{Conclusion}
This work presents a representation-consistent Fourier extension of the public Trans-SAR backbone for SAR intensity despeckling. Full-spectrum real--imaginary refinement is applied at six reconstruction scales, while a bounded log estimate is analytically returned to intensity before residual compensation. On the UCM-21 simulated test, the complete model obtains the highest macro PSNR and SSIM point estimates, and controlled ablations show clear reductions when Fourier refinement or intensity compensation is removed. On parent-disjoint real patches, encoder-frozen masked adaptation improves the M-index and edge preservation while reducing the extreme smoothness of the base output. Complementary scene evaluations show PSNR/SSIM gains over noisy inputs in all three UCMerced categories and consistent M-index/EPI improvements after AMS in all three real-SAR categories. The scope remains normalized single-image intensity restoration within the evaluated datasets; broader real-SAR claims require independent acquisitions and uncertainty analysis.
